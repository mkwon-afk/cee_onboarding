Getting Started

Two prerequisites before you can use any support
🔑
Active Login
A verified Red Hat account linked to your org's account number. Required to open cases and access entitlements.
✅
Active Subscription
A valid, unexpired Red Hat subscription. All support entitlements — cases, SLA, portal tools — require an active subscription.
1
Check your Order Fulfillment Notification Email
Sent automatically after purchase — review it before anything else
📧

This email contains your Account Number and Contract Number — both required to link your login and activate support.

📋 Account Number
Links your login to the right organization. Provided by Red Hat or your reseller at purchase.
📄 Contract Number
Tied to your order. Keep it safe — needed for renewals and future support requests.
View a sample order fulfillment email ▾
Subject: Your Red Hat order has been processed Dear Red Hat Customer, Purchase Order # xxxx Account Number: XXXXXX Reseller Name: XXX (if purchased via reseller) Order Details: Product Name: Red Hat Enterprise Linux for Virtual Datacenters with Satellite, Standard Contract Number: XXXXXXX Service Period: 20YY/MM/DD – 20YY/MM/DD Quantity: 1 → Get started: http://www.redhat.com/start → Customer Portal: http://access.redhat.com/subscriptions
Purchased through a reseller? Contact your reseller for order questions. For product or service questions, contact Red Hat Customer Service ↗.
2
Log in to the Customer Portal
access.redhat.com — your hub for cases, subscriptions, and tools
🌐

Use your account number from the fulfillment email to log in. If no login exists yet, watch for a separate Login Creation email — that's the only way to create a login linked to your account.

❓ No Login Creation email?
A login is likely already linked. Use account recovery ↗ to find your ID or reset your password.
Enter your login ID — not your email — when resetting a password.
🏢 Corporate or shared account?
Contact your org administrator to request access. If Auto-User Registration is enabled, you can self-connect via an org-specific link.
✅ Confirm you're on the right account: After logging in, click the Avatar icon (top-right) and verify your login ID matches the account number in your fulfillment email. Need help? Red Hat Customer Service ↗

e.g. RHEL 9.2, OpenShift 4.14, Satellite 6.12
✓
Product version
e.g. JBoss EAP 7.4, Ansible AAP 2.4
✓

✓

Severity Levels

Match your severity to your business impact — it determines who responds and how fast
🔴
SEV 1
Urgent
Production is completely down. Business-critical operations actively interrupted.
⚡ Immediate  ·  24×7
🟠
SEV 2
High
Service is partially functional. Actively impacting business operations on a critical system.
⏩ Accelerated
🟡
SEV 3
Medium
Currently working, but you've seen degradation or are monitoring early warning signs.
📋 Normal queue
⚪
SEV 4
Low
Non-urgent question or request. No interruption to business-critical operations.
💬 General query
Severity can be changed any time — by you or by Red Hat — as business impact evolves. A Sev 3 monitoring a worrying issue can become Sev 1 if it develops into a critical outage. Keep it updated.  Official policy ↗
Need coverage outside business hours? See 24×7 After-Hours Coverage ↑ — Sev 1 included by default, Sev 2 by request.

Escalation

If something isn't moving — use this

Escalation is a feature, not a failure. If your case stalls, business impact grows, or you need more visibility, Red Hat's Escalation Management Team steps in — pulling in the right people, aligning on a plan, and driving your issue to resolution. You should use it when you need it.

Signs it's time to escalate

SignalAction
No meaningful update after a reasonable period at current severityRequest escalation via the portal (RME)
Business impact has grown since the case was openedUpdate severity and request escalation
SLA breach is imminent or has occurredUse RME button or call the Escalation Hotline (Premium)
Multiple teams need to coordinate and progress has stalledRequest a cross-team escalation meeting via RME

Before you escalate — three things you need in place

✓  Support case open
A support case must exist before an escalation can be requested. Open one at access.redhat.com/support/cases if you haven't already.
✓  Severity set correctly
Severity must reflect actual business impact. Overstating severity does not yield faster results and may delay routing to the right team.
✓  Problem clearly defined
Document a clear problem description, business impact, and the environments affected. This is what the escalation team acts on.

How to trigger an escalation

Option 1 — Request Management Escalation (RME) Recommended

Open your support case on the Customer Portal and click "Request an escalation".

Use RME when the support case workflow is not meeting expectations or your case needs heightened attention. The Escalation Management Team will engage the right resources and coordinate efforts across Red Hat.

Option 2 — Escalation Hotline Premium · Sev 1 only

For mission-critical Severity 1 issues with a Premium subscription, call the dedicated 24×7 Escalation Hotline:

+1 919-890-8001

Have your case number ready. This line is for serious interruptions to business-critical operations only.

Additional escalation advocates

In parallel with RME, you can engage these contacts as internal advocates within Red Hat.

Technical Account Manager (TAM)
Your named TAM can escalate internally and coordinate across teams. Available within TAM business hours.
Customer Success Executive (CSE)
Your CSE can apply executive-level visibility to a stalled escalation and ensure cross-team accountability.
Sales Team
Your Red Hat account team can escalate to the appropriate leadership when business relationship impact is at stake.
Remote sessions: For complex issues, your support engineer may offer a secure remote session to diagnose together in real time. Request one proactively at access.redhat.com/articles/255443.
